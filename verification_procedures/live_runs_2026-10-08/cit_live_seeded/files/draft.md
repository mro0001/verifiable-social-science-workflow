# Why volunteers leave municipal programs

Municipal volunteer programs lose many recruits in their first year. In a panel of 48 city programs, 31% of first-year volunteers left before completing six months of service [@abara2019, p. 7]. Exit is steepest among volunteers recruited through online portals [@ferrante2021]. Castellanos and Ibe describe retention as a matter of fit between the volunteer's goals and the tasks offered [@castellanos2017].

## What programs do

Brennan puts it plainly: "volunteers leave when the work stops feeling like their own" [@brennan2016, p. 12]. Programs that cut orientation to a single session saw retention fall by 42% within a year [@kessler2019]. Volunteers who received written role descriptions stayed longer than those who did not [@okafor2018, p. 4].

Marchetti calls the records volunteers leave behind "civic paradata" [@marchetti2012]. Recognition events reduced exit among long-serving volunteers [@nakamura2020]. Supervisors' feedback quality predicted whether volunteers returned for a second season [@lindqvist2020]. Volunteers in smaller programs reported stronger attachment to staff [@haldane2017].

## What we know less about

Exit interviews are rarely conducted in municipal programs [@pruitt2022]. Peer mentoring was associated with higher retention in youth volunteer programs [@iversen2018]. Whitlock found that assigning a named staff contact kept volunteers longer in all three cities studied [@whitlock2020, p. 112]. Turnover among volunteer coordinators tends to precede spikes in volunteer exit [@sorensen2022]. Clear end dates for assignments made volunteers more willing to sign up again [@dumont2014]. Training length alone did not predict retention [@tanaka2016]. Quinlan reports that 57% of volunteers named scheduling conflicts as their main reason for leaving [@quinlan2015].
